00 / Position

From signal to system

I got here from the physical layer. Electronics and C first, then a long relationship with GNU/Linux, then eleven years keeping a 24/7 live television signal on air — where the failure is already on screen and the time budget is the length of the incident. That is still the standard I hold a system to: it has to survive being left alone, and someone who did not install it has to be able to operate it.

At AlterMundi — a twelve-person organisation building free infrastructure for community networks — I ran the infrastructure, helped stand up an independent Autonomous System, and architected SAI, a wildfire early-detection pilot whose edge nodes watched the Córdoba hills unattended for months. Across those systems I keep architecture, constraints, review, verification, deployment and operation explicit.

I am now building an independent applied-AI practice focused on researching the right solution before building, automating business processes, and practical AI training. The proposition is new; the engineering record behind it is not.

SAI — distributed wildfire early detection (validated pilot)

Architect and lead developer · 2024-11 – 2026-02

Architect and lead developer of a distributed wildfire early-detection system: edge nodes running computer-vision inference in the field, with fleet management, ingestion and dashboard on top. Three nodes ran continuously for months in Córdoba; field evaluation at the La Rancherita node reached F1 ≈ 0.86 in situ. The detection model was a specialist's work; I built and operated the system around it — edge fleet, ingestion pipeline, inference service and dashboard. My work on the funded pilot concluded when its funding cycle ended. AGPL-3.0.
